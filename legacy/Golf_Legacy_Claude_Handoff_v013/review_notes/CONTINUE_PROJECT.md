# Continue Golf Legacy

## Current state
This v0.6 working copy builds on the preserved v0.3 simulation, with diagnostic v0.4 and v0.5 experiments in between. The original conversation and Library v0.3 file were not overwritten. Start with START_HERE.md, then the change report and frozen configuration. Python source and exports, not prose, define the tested behavior.

## Design direction
Build a believable living professional-golf world, not predetermined champions or dramatic events. Skill, developed ability, form, ranking and opportunity are distinct. Elite players should sustain an advantage while credible upsets remain. Careers need lesser tours, setbacks, funded opportunities, hiatus/returns and varied decisions about continuing. Actual multi-season progress can justify more time; no automatic retirement birthday. Annual recaps must cite recorded events, never fabricate causal narratives.

## Implemented
Nonlinear upper-tail execution skill; mean-preserving residual-variance calibration; coherent veteran initialization; corrected familiarity, major experience and slump recovery; conserved tied-place payouts; auditable cash-gated entry and funded development; lightweight outside-work contribution and opportunity-cost decisions. Sponsorship is optional and OFF. See test suites and final held-out runs for scope.

## Research interpretation
Current top-125 OWGR history is survivor-conditioned, not the full professional funnel. OWGR rank is not a skill rating. Tier participation is not an official tour card. Older feeder-tour players and late arrivals must remain possible. Current ability and historical major concentration are calibration references, not quotas.

## Latest focused corrections
The v0.5 worlds exposed extreme early dominance and too few mini-tour opportunities. v0.6 adds a smooth extreme-skill taper, five regional mini circuits, earned premier eligibility after major wins and exclusive course/week scheduling. Added regional purses are explicit; a purse-neutral pilot confirms access to measurable progress without guaranteeing positive finances. The inherited broad rare-readiness profile and flat cross-region costs remain open calibration questions.

## Next priorities
1. Review the two final worlds' age, dominance, finances and entrant/cohort diagnostics before changing parameters again. Separate initialization warm-up from later entrants and account for censoring.
2. Validate economic cost/work/savings assumptions with better accessible evidence and sensitivity tests. Avoid expanding sponsor-management or other systems without a clear benefit.
3. Improve earned access and retention pathways: actual eligibility, qualifying and temporary starts should eventually replace simplified percentile promotions. Do not claim modeled tiers reproduce real card rules.
4. Consider player-facing annual recap presentation using the existing evidence schema; do not add arbitrary event lotteries to manufacture stories.
5. Run new seeds after any meaningful model change, retain frozen comparisons, audit ledgers/purses/starts/cuts and track uncertainty rather than tuning to one world's desired champion count.

No paid APIs or services are needed to run the simulation and tests. Research collection scripts are optional and require network availability; bundled raw third-party datasets are intentionally omitted.
