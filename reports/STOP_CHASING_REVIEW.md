## Findings

- **Monotone and smooth.** In every row more money means less pressure, and progress and a secured major
  place each lower it; no step anywhere is a cliff. A broke mini-tour professional with no progress stops
  about three times in four; with strong progress about one in three, and with a major place secured as
  well, one in five: improving players do accept losing seasons.
- **What the last change did.** Widening the broke term from `1 - 2 x runway` to `1 - runway` changes
  nothing at zero runway or above one season; it roughly doubles the chance only for golfers with between
  a tenth and a season of their own money (e.g. $30,000, no progress, mini tour: 14% to 29%). It was made
  so a professional who cannot pay for the next season does not linger unable to enter events; it is not
  tuned to one assertion (`test_no_debt_cliff` checks only that there is no cliff).
- **Debt below zero adds no pressure.** Runway is floored at zero, so $20,000 of debt weighs the same as
  an empty account. Spending is already blocked by the credit limit; whether deeper debt should push
  harder is an owner decision (not changed).
- **Uncalibrated.** The weights are assumptions, as before; the long run shows what they produce.
