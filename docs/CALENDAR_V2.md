# The reference calendar (v2): routes, rules and migration notes

Roadmap v14, chapter "Calendar and qualification rework". This is what the build does, where it differs from the
classic calendar, what was deliberately left out, and how an existing world would ever be moved (it is not).
The before/after list of every series is in [CALENDAR_MANIFEST.md](CALENDAR_MANIFEST.md) (generated by
`tools/calendar_manifest.py`).

## Versions, and who gets which

| | Classic (v1) | Reference (v2) |
|---|---|---|
| Who | every world started before this build, for good | new worlds (the default in "Create a world"; v1 can still be chosen for comparison) |
| Majors | weeks 18, 24, 25, 30 | weeks 15, 20, 25, 30 (five weeks apart) |
| Where it is defined | `golfsim/schedule.py` `V1` (frozen) | `golfsim/schedule.py` `V2` |

* One specification per version (`schedule.Spec`): major anchors, the 36 Premier weeks, fixed anchors (amateur,
  college, club professional), qualifying waves and finals, Q-School weeks and which award next season's cards,
  every ranking cut-off week, the post-major opportunity weeks, city pools for place-named events. The calendar
  (`calendar.WorldCalendar(seed, version)`), the course library (`course_library.default_library(version)`), the
  major rules (`qualification.rules_for(version)`) and the season engine all read it.
* **v1 is byte-identical to the calendar before this change**: the series list hashes to the same value
  (`tests/test_calendar_v2.py`), and a classic world plays out exactly as on the previous build (results, scores,
  prizes, golfers, entry routes, cards and world ranking compared over 112 weeks of a small world).
* A season records its calendar (`calendar` in each season file; `calendar` in the history's `world.json`); loading
  a world never changes its version. A save from before versions is v1.
* Stable identities: every series keeps its id. Only the qualifying waves were named by week in v1
  (`USA_OPEN_LOCAL_W19`); v2 names waves (`USA_OPEN_LOCAL_1`). `schedule.ALIASES` maps one onto the other.

The 36 Premier events (8 flagship, 20 standard including national opens, 8 opportunity) are at the reference
weeks; no two flagships share a week. Events named for a place are played there (Texas Open in Texas, Gulf
Coast Classic on the Gulf, Desert Classic in the desert South-West, Carolina, Southern, Great Lakes, Pacific
Northwest and Mid-Atlantic events in their regions). Every other tour, local amateur series, club professional
circuit, college season and national championship is generated around the fixed structure by the same rules as
before (same counts, fields, formats, country allocations and climate windows), so all of them keep playing,
including in the four major weeks.

## Qualification (v2)

**Cut-offs are immutable snapshots.** A ranking or points rule reads the list published at the end of its week
exactly (`QualificationContext.snapshot`). Asking for one not yet published raises `FutureSnapshot`; a later list
never stands in for it, nor an earlier one (v1 kept reading "the latest list on or before" the week). Each
season file keeps the snapshots the rules read (`cutoffs`: Masters 13, PGA 18, USA 20 and 24, Britannia 21 and
29, year end 52, and the amateur award).

**Amateur titles go to the edition their date allows.**

| Title | Feeds |
|---|---|
| USA Amateur champion / runner-up (week 33) | Masters, USA Open, Britannia Open (champion) of next season; champion and runner-up are separate categories (M4 / M4R, U2 / U2R) |
| Britannia Amateur (week 26) | this season's Britannia Open (B9); next season's USA Open (U4) and Masters (M5) -- never the USA Open played the week before it |
| USA Mid-Amateur, Asia-Pacific Amateur | next season's majors, as before |
| Latin America Amateur (week 3) | this season's Masters (M7) and Britannia Open |
| College national champion (week 22) | next season's Masters (M16, new; amateurs only) |
| Annual amateur award (after week 33) | next season's USA Open (UA) and Britannia Open (B13), while still an amateur; not a Masters route; nobody is named for a season before the world began |

Every amateur route requires amateur status when the field is set: turning professional ends that place (the
ledger records "expired: turned professional"); a conditional amateur place is never a professional card.

**Applying is separate from qualifying (USA Open and Britannia Open).** Golfers apply once, when the first wave
is entered, and each applicant picks one wave: one attempt (USA waves 17/19/21, Britannia waves 26/27). Only
exemptions already earned count: a cut-off not yet published is *pending*, so a projected top-60 place never
removes anyone's attempt. Before every stage the earned exemptions are checked again; an applicant who has since
become exempt, or cannot play that week, withdraws (recorded, with the reason) and the next applicant for that
wave takes the place. Local advancers go to the domestic final (USA week 24; Britannia final week 28); places
given up there go to first alternates in local finishing order (never a past score moved). The USA international
final (week 22) is a direct-entry route only; local qualifiers are never routed to it. Everything is in
`stage_log` (applied, wave chosen, withdrawn and why, advanced, alternates).

**One place, every reason.** A golfer satisfying several categories takes one place, with every category
recorded (as before). Exact slot counts use the recorded finishing order for ties (the club professionals' top
20: exactly 20 places, ties for the last ones settled by the recorded order -- the engine's stand-in for a
playoff), then ordered alternates.

**The rights ledger.** Every major place, both versions (observation only: it never decides a field):
golfer, categories (codes), state (earned / conditional), evidence (the source edition and result, ranking list
or award), when it was earned, target edition, the calendar version, and how it ended: *entered*, *withdrawn*
(injured or retired), *declined* (could not pay the trip; only before rules 2026-11-01, see below) or
*expired* (an amateur's place after turning professional; not eligible when the field was set). From rules
2026-11-01 each place decided when the field is set also keeps the money it was decided on (what the golfer could
draw, what was set aside, what remained, the trip's cost, who paid); an earned place is never lost for money
alone: if the golfer cannot pay, the organizer pays the trip's travel and entry in kind, recorded once per trip
in `travel_grants` (week, event, golfer, amount, what it covers, source) and never in the golfer's ledger.
Places already earned for next season are recorded at the season's end (and the amateur award in week 34),
then resolved when that field is set. Season files keep
`major_rights` (this season's, resolved) and `next_major_rights`; the event page of a major shows the ledger.

**Q-School.** The Asian Challenger Q-School moved from week 2 to week 51: it uses this season's standings and its
cards are for the next season, awarded once at the season's end. Every circuit championship finishes by week 48,
before its school.

**Travel inside a week (v2).** Two events in one week only if their days do not overlap and there is time to get
from one to the other: no gap needed within a country, one free day within a region, two between regions. A
Monday qualifier in England and a Thursday start in the USA work; a Tuesday final in England and a Thursday start
in the USA do not; a local qualifier on Monday and a college regional Monday to Wednesday never both happen.

## What is not in this build (deferred or decision-pending)

* **No old-world upgrade.** No world is moved from v1 to v2. A future migration would be a separately approved,
  season-boundary change on a copy: the archive already records each season's calendar version and every
  edition's played week, which is what such a migration needs; it would also need an untouched backup with a
  checksum, a rollback path and a report of future schedule changes, and must not rerun any result. If old dates
  could not be reconstructed exactly, the world should stay on v1.
* **"Projected / still able to qualify" views.** The ledger records earned and conditional places and how they
  ended; separate projections (who is currently inside a cut-off that is not yet published) are not shown.
* **Day-ordered engine.** The first safe release keeps every dependent stage in an earlier week (the validation
  enforces it); no Monday result feeds a same-week Thursday field.
* **College national champion into the same season's USA Open.** The roadmap allows it "if the chosen game
  category permits it"; no such category exists, so it was not added (the champion's route is next season's
  Masters).
* **Per-tour standings-lock / rights-effective table.** Q-School weeks, which schools award next season's cards,
  and circuit championships finishing by week 48 are in the specification and validated; a full per-tour cut-off
  table is not.
* **Full-size performance of v2.** Not measured: no long or full-size simulation was authorised. Small-world
  seasons run at the same speed as v1 (see the build report).
* **Parent/child tour filter.** Awaiting Ryan's decision; not implemented.

## Where it is tested

`tests/test_calendar_v2.py` (22 tests): v1 frozen (hash); anchors and counts; feeder order; venues, climate and
lower tours in major weeks; Q-School weeks; identities; future snapshots fail; projected rankings never remove an
attempt; Britannia Amateur year routing; champion and runner-up separate and status checked; duplicate routes
take one place; the award and the first season; the last club professional places; the Masters winner window
from the actual prior edition; evidence; travel; and one small v2 season reopened (majors' weeks, one attempt,
exempt players never use a qualifying place, no overlapping starts or impossible trips, award and ledger
resolved, season file records calendar and cut-offs). Classic-week tests elsewhere are pinned to v1.
